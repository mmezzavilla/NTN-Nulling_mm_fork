Thesis · STEP 1 progress report

Can a gNB beacon reach every VSAT it could harm?

15 Oct 2026, updated 22 Oct 2026 · branch step1-beacon-detection @ 27900a1b · Boulder scene, 10 GHz, 100 MHz · 20 drops × 300 VSATs

The thesis authenticates the TN–NTN nulling handshake. The gNB broadcasts a beacon carrying a random nonce, each VSAT that hears it replies with a ZKP-authenticated response, and the gNB nulls only toward authenticated VSATs. A VSAT that misses the beacon never replies and is never protected. STEP 1 asks how long that beacon must be so that every VSAT the gNB could harm decodes it.

  • 15–18 %
    of all VSATs are harmed but miss a beacon that fits in the paper's 20 µs preamble. That is 39–64 % of the harmed VSATs, at every power level.
  • Power ✕
    Raising TX power from 30 to 45 dBm does not fix it. Beacon SNR and data interference scale together, so the number of missed-but-harmed VSATs barely moves.
  • 2 ms
    The analytic length bound cuts the missed-but-harmed share to about 1 % of all VSATs; 10.7 ms cuts it to 0.2–0.3 %. Covering every one of the 6,000 placements takes 202 ms.
  • −27 dB
    The long tail comes from dips in the quasi-omni beacon pattern, not from noise or frequency fading. Wideband and carrier-only channels give the same tail.
Update · 22 Oct 2026

Cycling four beacon patterns closes the tail: 202 ms → 3.6 ms

The 15 Oct results traced the long tail to dips in the quasi-omni pattern (section 4.5). The fix tested here: send the beacon in K equal segments, each with the same Zadoff-Chu pattern shifted by half a DFT bin, so the dips of one pattern fall where the others are strong. Total energy and airtime are unchanged. Each segment carries its own preamble, so the receiver pays a small non-coherent combining cost, which the link-level simulation measures.

What this shows

  • The diagnosis was right. Removing the pattern dips removes the tail. With K = 4, every one of the 6,000 placements decodes within 3.6 ms, a 56× shorter beacon than before, and the 99 % point now sits below the analytic bound.
  • The cost is negligible. Four segments with their own preambles need 0.13 dB more energy than one (24.45 vs 24.32 dB). Two segments cost 0.03 dB.
  • Both axes need filling. K = 2 shifts only along the 16-element (azimuth) axis. The 4-element elevation axis has the widest gaps between DFT beams, and its dips remain, so the tail stays. K = 4 covers both axes.
  • The tail is now bounded, not just short. From T_req/T_bound = (INR_th/INR)/(3ρ), the worst VSAT can need at most 1/(3ρ_min) ≈ 1.8 × the bound, which is 3.8 ms with ρ_min = −7.3 dB. With K = 1 there was no such ceiling, and the "all harmed" length kept growing with every new drop.
  • Airtime. A 3.6 ms beacon on 3 time-multiplexed sectors is about 11 ms per beacon period: 1.1 % of airtime at one beacon per second.

1 · The problem, flipped

In the baseline paper (Jia et al.) the VSAT transmits and the gNB detects it with 64 antennas. Here the direction is reversed: the gNB transmits a beacon and each single-antenna VSAT must detect it and decode the 128-bit nonce. Two facts shape the problem.

  • The beacon cannot be beamformed. The gNB does not know where the VSATs are, so it sends the beacon quasi-omni from all 64 elements. A VSAT receives roughly the single-element gain, ‖h‖²/N_tx, while a data beam aimed at it delivers up to N_tx = 64 times more (18 dB).
  • Beacon and data share the same power P. Both the beacon SNR and the interference a VSAT suffers scale with P. Power therefore changes how many VSATs are at risk, but not whether the at-risk ones hear the beacon. Only the beacon length T, meaning its total energy, changes that.

Success criterion

Every VSAT is coloured by comparing two quantities.

harmed : INR_worst = Σ_s P·‖h_s‖² / (kT·NF·B) ≥ −6 dB (all 3 sector beams on the VSAT) decoded : γ_s = P·T·|wᴴh_s|² / (kT·NF) ≥ γ_req for at least one sector s

green = decoded the nonce from at least one sector; red = harmed and decoded nothing (the failure we want to remove); grey = missed but harmless. "Decoded" means detected and nonce correct, not just detected. "Harmed" is a deliberately conservative worst case: every sector's full beam pointed at the VSAT at once, so the result does not depend on which TN-UE happens to be served.

Setting the two conditions against each other gives a closed-form rule. If the beacon pattern gave exactly ‖h_s‖²/N_tx, red dots vanish once

T ≥ T_bound = 3 · γ_req · N_tx / (INR_th · B) = 2.07 ms

The factor 3 covers harm summed over three sectors when the VSAT can decode only one. The paper's 20 µs preamble is about 100 times shorter.

4 · Results

4.1 Decoding the nonce, not detecting the beacon, sets the energy budget

The nonce costs about 3 dB more than detection at the 99 % point. In energy-per-bit terms, γ_req ≈ 270 spread over 128 bits is about 2.1 per information bit (3.2 dB Eb/N0, preamble included). That is a sensible operating point for a rate-1/4 LDPC code. Because the success curve depends only on total energy and not on T, one number, γ_req, carries the whole link layer into the maps.

4.2 Maps: the 20 µs beacon leaves many harmed VSATs unprotected

Two patterns stand out. Going down a column (more power), green grows but red does not shrink, because new VSATs enter the harmed set as fast as old ones become audible. Going right along a row (longer beacon), red turns green. At 45 dBm and 10.7 ms the drop has no red at all. The grey majority are mostly VSATs with no ray-traced path to the gNB (39 % of placements) or with weak sidelobe coupling.

The harmed share grows with power, from 28 % to 41 %. The missed-but-harmed share at 20 µs stays at 15–18 % regardless. This is the expected result: power moves the boundary of the at-risk region, length decides whether the beacon covers it.

4.4 How long must the beacon be?

The median harmed VSAT needs only 6–50 µs, so the bulk is easy. The trouble is the tail. Between the analytic bound (2 ms) and full coverage (≈ 200 ms) the missed share falls only from about 3 % to zero, and the last 1 % alone costs a factor of 20 in length. The wideband and carrier-only curves overlap. Averaging over 100 MHz removes frequency fading but does not change the tail, so fading is not its cause.

4.5 Why the tail exists: pattern dips in the quasi-omni beacon

The required length of each harmed VSAT, relative to the bound, factors into two terms:

T_req / T_bound = (INR_th / INR_worst) · 1 / (3ρ)

The first term is the interference margin. Most harmed VSATs are far above threshold (median INR +7 dB at 30 dBm, +16 dB at 45 dBm), and every dB of excess harm is a dB of excess beacon SNR, so they decode early. The second term is the pattern loss. A VSAT exceeds the bound only when it sits near the harm threshold and in a dip of the ZC quasi-omni pattern. The ZC precoder is flat on the 64 DFT beam directions but not between them, and with sparse multipath (a few strong rays) the VSAT sees that ripple directly. The red that remains at the bound is therefore a property of the precoder, and the precoder is something we can change.

5 · Early conclusions

  1. The authenticated-beacon approach is viable at the PHY layer, with a beacon of a few milliseconds. A 2 ms beacon per sector protects 96–98 % of the harmed VSATs, and 10.7 ms protects 99 %. With one beacon per second on three time-multiplexed sectors, that is 0.6 % to 3.2 % of gNB airtime.
  2. A beacon the length of the paper's 20 µs preamble is not enough. It leaves 15–18 % of all VSATs harmed and unprotected. A spoof-resistant beacon carries a 128-bit nonce, and decoding it needs about 3 dB more energy than detection alone.
  3. Length is the design lever, not power. The missed-but-harmed share is flat across 30–45 dBm, as the scaling argument predicts. Raising power only grows the set of VSATs that need protecting.
  4. The analytic bound T = 3γ_req·N_tx / (INR_th·B) is a good engineering rule. It predicts the 2 ms knee from three numbers. In the ray-traced scene it leaves about 1 % of all VSATs (2–4 % of the harmed ones) unprotected.
  5. Full coverage is limited by the quasi-omni pattern, not by the link. Closing the last 1 % with length alone costs 100× airtime. Update 22 Oct: cycling 4 half-bin-shifted patterns closes it, with full coverage at 3.6 ms (see the update at the top).

Caveats to keep in mind

  • Harm is worst case. All three sector beams aim at each VSAT simultaneously. Real data beams follow TN-UEs, so the true harmed set is smaller and the red shares above are upper bounds.
  • No-path VSATs count as harmless. 39 % of placements receive no ray within depth 3 and are grey by construction. Diffraction or deeper reflections could move some of them into the harmed set.
  • The link level is idealised. AWGN with perfect timing and frequency sync. The wideband gain average treats the frequency-selective channel as if coding captured its mean energy, which is slightly optimistic for the deepest-faded VSATs.
  • Statistics. 20 drops versus the paper's 100. The 99 % point is stable, but the "all harmed" length is the maximum over 6,000 samples and will grow with more drops.
